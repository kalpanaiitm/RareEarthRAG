# Demo note 05: Molybdates, antimonites and mixed-anion host frameworks

Written for the RareEarthRAG demo. This is an illustrative teaching note, not a published paper.

Rare-earth molybdates are well known luminescent hosts. In many molybdates the molybdenum is tetrahedrally coordinated as MoO4 groups, and scheelite-related structures are common. These hosts show a strong oxygen-to-molybdenum charge-transfer absorption band in the ultraviolet, and energy can transfer from this band to Eu3+ dopants, which makes Eu3+-doped molybdates candidate red phosphors.

Antimonites contain antimony in the +3 oxidation state. The Sb3+ ion has a stereochemically active 5s2 lone pair, so SbO3 units adopt a trigonal pyramidal shape. Lone-pair cations are of interest because they tend to produce asymmetric coordination environments and, in some cases, non-centrosymmetric crystal structures.

Mixed-anion frameworks that combine molybdate groups and antimonite units with rare-earth ions, sometimes described as molybdoantimonites, bring together these features: a rare-earth emitting centre, a molybdate charge-transfer absorber and a lone-pair cation that can lower the local symmetry. A low-symmetry site can strengthen hypersensitive transitions such as the Eu3+ 5D0 to 7F2 red emission.

Determining such structures usually needs single-crystal X-ray diffraction, supported by powder XRD for phase purity.
