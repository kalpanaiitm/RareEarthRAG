# Demo note 06: Applications of rare-earth materials and supply concerns

Written for the RareEarthRAG demo. This is an illustrative teaching note, not a published paper.

Rare-earth phosphors are central to modern lighting and displays. A common white LED combines a blue indium gallium nitride chip with a yellow-emitting Ce3+-doped yttrium aluminium garnet (YAG:Ce) phosphor. Adding a red phosphor, often based on Eu3+ or Eu2+, improves the colour rendering of the white light.

Other applications include neodymium-iron-boron permanent magnets for motors and wind turbines, lanthanum-containing catalysts for petroleum refining, cerium oxide for polishing and catalysis, and gadolinium compounds as contrast agents in magnetic resonance imaging. Lanthanide-doped materials are also used as security inks, bio-imaging probes and optical thermometers.

Supply is a recurring concern. Rare-earth elements occur together in minerals such as bastnaesite and monazite, and separating them is difficult because of their chemical similarity. Solvent extraction with many stages is the standard industrial separation method. Several governments list rare-earth elements as critical raw materials, which motivates research into recycling and into reducing the rare-earth content of devices.
