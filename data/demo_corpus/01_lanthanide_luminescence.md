# Demo note 01: Luminescence of trivalent lanthanide ions

Written for the RareEarthRAG demo. This is an illustrative teaching note, not a published paper.

Trivalent lanthanide ions such as Eu3+, Tb3+, Sm3+ and Dy3+ emit light through transitions within the 4f shell. Because the 4f orbitals are shielded by the filled 5s and 5p orbitals, the emission lines are sharp and their positions change only slightly from one host lattice to another. The 4f-4f transitions are parity forbidden, so excited-state lifetimes are long, typically in the millisecond range.

Europium (Eu3+) gives red emission. The 5D0 to 7F2 transition near 612 to 615 nm is an electric-dipole transition that is hypersensitive to the local environment, while the 5D0 to 7F1 transition near 590 nm is magnetic-dipole in character and largely insensitive to the site. The intensity ratio of these two lines is widely used as a probe of site symmetry: a large ratio indicates that the Eu3+ ion occupies a site without an inversion centre.

Terbium (Tb3+) gives green emission, dominated by the 5D4 to 7F5 transition near 545 nm. Cerium (Ce3+) behaves differently: its emission comes from the 5d to 4f transition, which is broad and strongly affected by the host crystal field.

When the dopant concentration is too high, energy migrates between neighbouring lanthanide ions until it reaches a quenching site, and the emission intensity falls. This concentration quenching sets an optimum doping level for each phosphor.
