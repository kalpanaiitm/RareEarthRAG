# Demo note 04: Lanthanide contraction and crystal structures

Written for the RareEarthRAG demo. This is an illustrative teaching note, not a published paper.

The rare-earth elements comprise the fifteen lanthanides from lanthanum to lutetium, together with scandium and yttrium. Across the lanthanide series the ionic radius of the trivalent ion decreases steadily from La3+ to Lu3+. This lanthanide contraction arises because 4f electrons shield one another poorly from the increasing nuclear charge.

Because the ions are chemically similar but steadily smaller, a family of rare-earth compounds often keeps the same crystal structure for the larger ions and switches to a different structure type for the smaller ones. Coordination numbers also tend to fall across the series, from eight or nine for the early lanthanides to six or seven for the late ones.

Yttrium (Y3+) has an ionic radius close to that of Ho3+, which is why yttrium compounds are often used as optically inactive hosts that can be doped with emitting lanthanides. Gadolinium and lanthanum compounds are used in the same way.

Crystal structures are commonly described in terms of linked coordination polyhedra, for example rare-earth oxygen polyhedra sharing edges or corners with tetrahedral or octahedral anion groups.
