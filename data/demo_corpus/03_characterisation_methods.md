# Demo note 03: Characterisation of rare-earth materials

Written for the RareEarthRAG demo. This is an illustrative teaching note, not a published paper.

Powder X-ray diffraction (powder XRD) is the first check on any new solid. The diffraction pattern confirms phase purity by comparison with reference patterns, and Rietveld refinement of the pattern gives lattice parameters and atomic positions. Single-crystal X-ray diffraction is used when crystals are large enough and provides the most reliable crystal structure.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) shows particle size and morphology, and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) gives an approximate elemental composition. Infrared and Raman spectroscopy identify characteristic vibrations, such as Mo-O stretching modes in molybdates.

Photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy records the emission spectrum under a fixed excitation wavelength, while the photoluminescence excitation (PLE) spectrum shows which wavelengths produce that emission. Decay curves give the excited-state lifetime, and the quantum yield measures how efficiently absorbed photons are converted into emitted light. CIE chromaticity coordinates are calculated from the emission spectrum to describe the perceived colour.

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and diffuse reflectance spectroscopy are also common, the latter giving an estimate of the optical band gap.
